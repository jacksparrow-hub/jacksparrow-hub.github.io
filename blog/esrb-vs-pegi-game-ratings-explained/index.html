ESRB vs PEGI Game Ratings: What the Labels Actually Tell You

You find two copies of a game: one says E10+ and the other says PEGI 7. Are they contradicting each other? Not necessarily. The US and UK commonly use different rating systems, with their own categories and criteria. The number or letter is a starting point, not a full account of what happens when someone plays.

Which label am I looking at?

The Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) publishes categories such as Everyone, Everyone 10+, Teen, Mature 17+, and Adults Only 18+. The category indicates the general age suitability of a game's content. ESRB also provides content descriptors and interactive-element notices.

PEGI's age labels are 3, 7, 12, 16 and 18. PEGI explains that its classifications are about age suitability, not how hard a game is to play. Its separate icons describe content and interactive risks. If you see a PEGI rating on a UK listing and an ESRB rating on a US listing, read each label on its own terms rather than trying to convert one into the other.

Question ESRB (common on US game listings) PEGI (common on UK game listings)
What is the broad age signal? A named category, such as Everyone 10+ or Teen A minimum-age label, such as PEGI 7 or PEGI 12
Where do I learn why? Content Descriptors, plus the rating summary when available Content descriptor icons and text
What about spending or online play? Interactive Elements such as In-Game Purchases and Users Interact Interactive risk descriptors, including In-Game Purchases

The descriptions above come from the ESRB ratings guide and PEGI label guide. The two systems do not publish an exact label-for-label equivalence in those guides, so this table is a guide to reading the labels, not a conversion chart.

Read beyond the age badge

An age badge cannot tell you everything about the play experience. ESRB says its Content Descriptors identify material that may have influenced a category or be of interest, but they are not intended as a complete list of a game's content. PEGI likewise uses descriptors to explain why a rating was given and to flag certain interactive risks. Open the game's own rating entry or store listing, then look at the descriptor text, not only the icon. (ESRB; PEGI)

Consider an imaginary game with a mild-looking age badge. If the listing also says In-Game Purchases, it offers paid items inside the game. ESRB's In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items) notice is more specific: it indicates offers where the buyer does not know the exact item before purchase, such as a loot box or mystery pack. ESRB describes Users Interact as possible exposure to unfiltered user-generated communication or media. These are feature notices; ESRB says its Interactive Elements do not change the assigned age category. (ESRB ratings guide)

PEGI's interactive risk descriptors also include In-Game Purchases, with a separate notice for paid random items in relevant cases. Read those notices before assuming a game with a low age number has no spending features. (PEGI label guide)

Three checks before installing or buying a game

  1. Find the specific game's rating. Search the rating body's database or read the exact platform's store page. Don't rely on a screenshot from another edition or region.
  2. Read the descriptor words. An icon for violence, fear, language or purchases tells you more than the age label alone. If something matters to your household, check the fuller rating text.
  3. Look separately at online features and spending. A content rating is not a promise that other players' chat or optional paid items are absent. Check the current store listing and game settings for the version you will use.

A rating is information for a decision, not a review score or a guarantee that everyone of the stated age will enjoy the game. PEGI explicitly distinguishes age suitability from difficulty. For ESRB's definitions and rating search, start with its ratings guide. For the UK-style labels and descriptor examples, use PEGI's guide.
