Does a Matter Smart Plug Show Energy Use in Google Home?

Short answer: A Matter badge and an “energy monitoring” label do not guarantee that Google Home will show watts or kilowatt-hours from the plug. Check three separate layers: what the exact plug and firmware report through Matter, what Google's current device support covers, and what its Home app displays. The maker's app may show readings even when Google Home does not. This is a documentation-based checklist, not a test of any plug or Google account.

What Matter 1.3 changed, and what it did not

The Connectivity Standards Alliance's Matter 1.3 announcement introduced a way for devices to report instantaneous power and energy consumed over time. It did not switch on energy reporting for every older Matter plug or require every smart-home app to display it. The plug maker must implement the feature, and the ecosystem must handle and expose those measurements. Check the model and firmware, not only the Matter logo or a package claim about monitoring.

Check 1: what does this exact plug report?

Look up the full model number and firmware release notes on the maker's site. “Energy monitoring” might mean readings only in the manufacturer's own app. Ask whether the maker explicitly says power and energy readings are exposed over Matter and which firmware enables that. Do not confuse a device's ability to switch on and off through Matter with its ability to share kWh through the same connection. To add a Matter-over-Wi-Fi plug to a chosen ecosystem, you need a compatible Matter controller in that ecosystem; a Matter-over-Thread plug also needs a Thread border router in its setup. Those hardware requirements are separate from energy-data support.

Check 2: what does Google actually support?

Google's Matter supported-device table distinguishes device types and their Google Assistant, Google Home app and display support; not all device types are fully supported. Its entries alone do not establish that Google Home will display energy measurements from a particular Matter smart plug. Read the current table for the exact device type and ask the maker what its Google Home integration surfaces for the model and firmware you own.

TP-Link's Matter feature matrix is a useful example of why app choice matters for the applicable TP-Link models listed there: it lists features across Alexa, Google, Apple, SmartThings and Tapo/Kasa separately and warns that supported features can change. Do not transfer a checkmark for one ecosystem to another. This is an example of checking a support matrix, not a recommendation or a claim that every TP-Link plug works the same way.

Check 3: where do you see the reading?

If the plug pairs in Google Home and turns on/off but no power or energy tile appears, first check the maker's app. For supported Tapo plugs, TP-Link's energy-usage instructions direct users to the Tapo app's product page, then Energy Usage and “Show More.” A reading in Tapo is evidence that the plug can measure energy locally or in its own ecosystem, not proof Google Home exposes it through Matter. Check firmware and the vendor's Google-specific support matrix before changing hubs or buying a replacement. If the display still differs, ask the maker which app and firmware combination is supported.

Before buying a plug for energy tracking

  • Read the full model and firmware requirements, then confirm whether energy data travels over Matter or only appears in the maker app.
  • Check the ecosystem and app where you actually want to see watts/kWh; do not equate “works with Google Home” with energy-chart support.
  • Check the electrical rating against your appliance and socket, and seek a qualified electrician for uncertain or high-load use. Energy monitoring does not make an unsuitable load safe.
  • Do not buy a Thread border router merely to fix an absent Google Home energy chart for a Matter-over-Wi-Fi plug.

Quick questions

Does Matter 1.3 make an old energy-monitoring plug show watts in Google Home?

No automatic guarantee. The plug needs appropriate firmware and the chosen platform and app need to support the relevant feature. Confirm the exact model's current support.

My plug works in Google Home. Why is there no kWh chart?

On/off control and energy data are different capabilities. Check the maker's app and Google-specific feature matrix before diagnosing a fault or buying hardware.

Is a maker-app reading the same as a Matter reading?

Not necessarily. A maker can expose measurements in its own app without showing them through another platform. Ask which path the vendor actually supports.
