How to Choose a New Smartphone: 7 Specs That Actually Matter

Phone ads are full of big numbers: megapixels, gigahertz, gigabytes. Most of them don't matter much in daily use. Here's what does, so you can pick the right phone without overpaying.

1. Price range first

Decide your budget before you look at features. Consumer Reports notes that a premium phone can easily cost $1,000 or more, while many good phones sell for well under $600 (Consumer Reports phone buying guide). Mid-range phones today handle messaging, video, maps and social apps with no trouble.

2. Software updates

This is the spec most people forget. A phone that gets security updates for many years stays safe and useful much longer. Before buying, check the maker's official update promise for that exact model (it's usually on the product page or support site). A cheaper phone with long support can be better value than a pricier one with short support.

3. Battery life

Look at real-world battery tests from reviewers rather than only the mAh number. Screen size, chip efficiency and software all change how long a battery lasts. If you use maps, video or games a lot, battery should be near the top of your list.

4. Camera (but not megapixels)

More megapixels don't mean better photos. What matters:

  • Low-light performance (photos indoors or at night)
  • An ultra-wide lens for groups and landscapes
  • Optical zoom if you often shoot far-away things

Compare sample photos in reviews, especially night shots.

5. Storage

Photos and videos fill phones fast. 128GB is a practical minimum for most people today; heavy video users should consider more. Check whether the phone has a memory card slot, since many don't.

6. 5G and your carrier

You need a 5G phone to use 5G, and the FCC advises checking with your mobile provider whether 5G service is available in your area before you buy (FCC 5G FAQs). Also make sure the phone supports your carrier's bands, which matters most if you buy an imported or unlocked model.

7. Size and build

A phone you enjoy holding matters more than a spec sheet. If possible, try it in a store. Look for water resistance ratings (like IP68) if you're rough on phones.

Specs you can mostly ignore

  • Processor clock speed (GHz): Real-world speed depends on the whole chip and software.
  • Huge RAM numbers: Beyond a sensible amount, extra RAM gives little everyday benefit.
  • Very high megapixel counts: See point 4.

Quick checklist before you buy

  1. Does it fit my budget?
  2. How many years of updates are promised?
  3. What do real battery tests say?
  4. How are the night photos?
  5. Enough storage for my photos and apps?
  6. Works with my carrier's 5G?
  7. Comfortable in my hand?
