Matter device won't connect to Google Home? Start here

Don't reset everything at once. First identify where the setup fails. This symptom checklist uses Google's published support steps; it is not a claim that we diagnosed or tested your particular device. A Matter device paired in its maker's app may need sharing into Google Home rather than a fresh setup.

Pick the matching symptom

What you seeFirst checks
The device is not found during first setup.Confirm power and pairing mode in the maker's manual. Use the current Google Home app and a phone with supported Bluetooth Low Energy. Verify your Matter hub; Thread devices need a Thread border router.
The QR code will not scan.Use Google's on-screen alternative to add another way and follow the maker's setup-code instructions; don't post or share a setup code publicly.
Setup freezes or fails to finish.Google's sequence is: restart the Matter device, close Home, clear the app cache where applicable, restart the phone, reopen Home and try again. If it still fails, contact the maker before a factory reset.
It worked at first, then control fails.Check the home network's IPv6 setting. Google's Matter hub guide warns that setup can appear to succeed while later control fails without IPv6.
It works in the maker's app but is missing from Google Home.Follow the maker app's sharing prompt or share flow for Google Home. An existing Matter device may be added through multi-admin rather than paired from scratch.

Google's primary references are its Matter setup and troubleshooting guide and hub and IPv6 preparation guide.

Before changing your router or buying a new hub

  1. Check the connection type for this model. Matter over Wi-Fi uses the home Wi-Fi network; Matter over Thread needs a Thread border router. Do not infer the transport from the Matter logo. The hub decision table shows why this matters.
  2. Check your controller. Google lists some hubs for Matter over Wi-Fi only and others for both Wi-Fi and Thread. Compare its list to your exact hub model; don't assume a Nest speaker has the same radios as a Nest display.
  3. Check IPv6 cautiously. Google's guide recommends enabling IPv6 on the home router and consulting its manual or your internet provider. Router screens differ. Avoid disabling other network settings speculatively.
  4. Check device-type support. If pairing succeeds but a particular control is absent, Google's device-type table distinguishes app, voice and display support. That is a different problem from failure to join the network.

When to stop and seek device help

If Google's restart and app-cache steps do not finish setup, its help says to contact the device manufacturer; a factory reset may be needed. A reset can erase a device's configuration and make you set it up again, so consult the model-specific manual first. If the issue is a manufacturer-app-to-Google sharing step, use our multi-admin guide and the maker's instructions. Do not publish a QR/setup code in a forum while asking for help.
